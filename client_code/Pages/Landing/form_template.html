How a hunt goes

The grown-up part takes five minutes. The kid part takes the rest of the afternoon.

  1. Snap the treasures

    Walk around the house and photograph 5 to 10 things: the teapot, a teddy, a stripy sock. Add a spoken clue if you like.

  2. Show the QR code

    Kids scan it with the tablet camera, type their name and pick a cake topper. A team can share one tablet.

  3. Hunt and snap

    They run off, find each thing and photograph it. Magic match compares the photos; close calls go to you.

  4. Blow out the candles

    A full cake, or the clock runs out: kids blow on the tablet to put the candles out, then win their stickers.

Four ways to play

Photo hunt

Kids see your photo of each treasure and hunt for the real thing. Best for 4 and up, and for party guests who don't know the house.

Voice hunt

Record a riddle in your own voice: "I have hands but no fingers." Kids listen, work it out and snap the answer. The photo stays secret until they find it.

Task party

Record tasks ("hop like a frog to the door!") or pick from 100 ready-made party tasks. Each one comes with a friendly recorded voice. Some have a timer, some want a silly photo.

Photo bingo

For little ones: 4, 6 or 9 big pictures on a bingo card. Tap a picture, hear its name, find it and snap it. A whole row is a BINGO! Try it with code BINGO9.

Everything a party needs

Magic match

The app compares each snap with your photo. Easy, just right or tricky, to suit the age.

Grown-up check

Close calls ask you. Say yes from your phone, or on the kid's tablet after a quick grown-ups-only sum.

Live board

Start the clock, add five minutes, watch every kid's candles light up as they find things.

Race or together

Everyone fills their own cake, or the whole party fills one cake as a team. With or without a timer.

Party album

Your photo next to every kid's snap of the same thing, plus printable certificates for each hunter.

Kids' corner and stickers

Each of your kids gets their own corner on the tablet: join with one tap, keep a sticker book.

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. Making hunts, playing them, the album and the 100 tasks are all free.

Do kids need an account?

No. Only the grown-up who makes the hunt has an account. Kids scan the code and type a first name or a team name. Your own kids can have a Kids' corner on the tablet, still without an account.

How does it know it's the same thing?

The app makes a small colour-and-shape fingerprint of your photo and of the kid's snap and compares them. It's forgiving about light and angle. When it isn't sure, it asks a grown-up, so nobody is stuck.

Who can see the photos?

Only you, the grown-up who made the hunt, and each kid sees their own snaps. Delete all the kids' photos of a hunt with one button in the album. Tip for kids: snap the thing, not the people.

What if the camera won't start?

Every camera screen also has "Choose a photo", which works with the tablet's own camera app. On a hunt in "every photo counts" mode, any picture lights a candle.

Can several kids share one tablet?

Yes: join as a team ("Zoe & Sam"). In a race each tablet is one team; in "together" mode all finds go on one cake.
